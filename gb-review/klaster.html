Polish Cluster for Community Heritage Protection

Fundacja Ruinersi na Dolnym Śląsku initiated and coordinates the Cluster. It is a network of civil-society organisations, public institutions, local authorities and private companies that pool experience, knowledge and resources to protect our heritage more effectively.

Shared goal

Why we join forces

The Cluster brings together organisations and institutions that look at heritage protection from different perspectives. We work together where a shared voice and exchange of knowledge can make a real difference. In practice, this means:

  • strengthening civil-society organisations and building networks
  • sharing experience and delivering joint projects
  • developing skills and supporting traditional crafts
  • sharing resources, creating documentation and developing good practice
  • working with academia and public administration
  • representing a community-led approach to heritage protection
  • developing solutions that can be used across Poland
Origins

From discussion to joint action

The idea for the Cluster grew out of conversations between organisations and community activists who work with historic buildings and heritage every day.

An important catalyst was the Kongres Konserwatorów Polskich. It made clear that NGOs, heritage owners and local communities need a stronger role in the heritage protection system. Ruinersi brought the perspective of private owners of old houses and people working directly on the ground into the discussion.

This was followed by a meeting at the Ducal Tower in Siedlęcin, where the agreement establishing the Cluster was signed. The congress was an important stimulus, but the Cluster itself is the result of a much longer process of collaboration.

Supporting Partners

Academia and institutions supporting the Cluster

Politechnika Wrocławska is a Supporting Partner of the Cluster. The collaboration includes analysis, studies, documentation, academic and student cooperation and — where possible — the use of facilities and equipment in joint projects.

Structure

How the Cluster works

The Cluster is not a separate institution but a network of independent organisations. Each member retains its autonomy while working with others where joint action is easier and more effective.

Cluster members

Member organisations contribute their own experience, knowledge, contacts and initiatives. They can propose joint activities and take part in selected projects.

Cluster Council

Representatives of member organisations agree the areas of collaboration, priorities and the most important joint activities.

Cluster coordinator

Fundacja Ruinersi na Dolnym Śląsku coordinates communication, organises collaboration, maintains documentation and helps turn agreements into concrete action.

Joint activities

For specific topics, interested members get involved. Not every organisation has to take part in everything — the Cluster brings together the right expertise where it is needed.

In practice: organisations remain independent → they agree the direction together → the coordinator helps turn decisions into action → members join projects according to their expertise.

Supporting Partners

Universities, institutions and other organisations can support the Cluster with knowledge, facilities, research or other resources.

Thematic working groups

Work can focus on specific areas including conservation, crafts, education, natural building, tourism and local development.
